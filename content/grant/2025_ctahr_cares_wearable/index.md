# Executive Summary

This CTAHR CARES pilot project develops **non-invasive wearable sweat sensors** to
monitor **heat stress in beef cattle** at the molecular level. By coupling
commercial sweat and internal temperature sensors with **sweat metabolomics** and
**gut microbiome profiling**, the team aims to uncover biomarkers that distinguish
**heat-stressed** from **heat-resistant** animals.

Heat stress in cattle is typically assessed using a combination of **visual
observation**, **physiological measurements**, and **environmental indices** such
as the Temperature–Humidity Index (THI). These methods are indirect, labor
intensive, and often fail to capture **individual animal differences** in
tolerance. This project will generate **continuous, individual-level data** on
sweating, body temperature, movement, and molecular signatures to enable
climate-resilient herd management and breeding strategies in Hawaiʻi and beyond.

# Problem Statement

Beef cattle in tropical and subtropical environments face increasingly frequent
**heat-stress events** driven by climate change. Current evaluation methods:

- Rely on **THI** and farm-level weather data  
- Use **intermittent measurements** of body temperature and respiration  
- Depend on **subjective observation** of panting, drooling, and behavior  

However:

- **Individual and breed variation** in heat tolerance is large  
- Early, sub-clinical heat stress is often **missed**  
- Invasive or sporadic measurements are **not scalable** for large herds  

There is a critical need for **fast, accurate, and non-invasive tools** to
determine when a specific cow is experiencing heat stress, and to identify
animals that remain resilient under extreme conditions.

# Research Objectives

**Overall Goal:** Develop an integrated framework that links **wearable sweat and
temperature sensing** with **metabolomics** and **microbiome** data to quantify
heat stress and heat resistance in cattle.

## Objective 1 – Characterize Heat Stress Physiology

- Relate **sweating rate**, internal body temperature, and movement patterns to
  **environmental conditions** (THI, day/night cycles, seasonal variation)  
- Capture how **apocrine sweat glands** in cattle respond under different levels
  of heat load  

## Objective 2 – Identify Molecular Biomarkers

- Perform **sweat metabolomics** to identify compounds associated with heat
  stress vs. heat resistance  
- Analyze **gut microbiota** (from fecal samples) and **blood-based markers**  
- Measure **thyroid hormone** levels, **heat shock proteins (HSPs)**, and
  **mitochondrial ATP-related gene expression** as potential indicators of
  chronic heat load  

## Objective 3 – Enable Climate-Resilient Cattle Selection

- Integrate physiological and molecular data to identify **candidate biomarkers
  and genetic markers** of heat stress and heat resistance  
- Provide a foundation for **future selection and breeding programs** targeting
  climate-resilient cattle in Hawaiʻi’s diverse environments
